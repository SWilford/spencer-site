## Confirmed scope

The portfolio remains unpublished. Individual playback history, event timestamps, original imports, exports, credentials, administration, and collector controls remain private. ## Implemented surface

The heading is **Music Stats**, with functional Stats and Top view buttons and a **Last collected** timestamp in Eastern time. The simplified surface has no explanatory subtitle, private-preview badge, refresh button, or visible duration-warning panel.

Both views share one sticky timeframe control. At extended widths (1280px and above), all eight choices appear directly: Day, Week, Month, Year, Lifetime, Past year, Year to date, and Custom. Normal widths keep the first five direct and place the last three in an anchored overflow menu. Compact widths (680px and below) keep Day, Month, and Lifetime direct and put the remaining five behind a three-line icon. Previous/next controls navigate day, week, month, and year. Weeks begin Monday, custom endpoints are inclusive, Past year covers 365 calendar dates, and current calendar periods end on the saved snapshot's Eastern date. The API validates dates and limits a requested range to 30 years. Changing the range hides the preceding range's results while the new request is pending or fails.

Stats includes:

- Eight metric tiles: streams, different tracks, minutes, different artists, hours, different albums, days of listening, and active days. Eligible non-Lifetime metrics show change against the preceding period of the same length when prior data supplies a denominator.
- Two 24-hour listening clocks, for streams and minutes, using America/New_York. Pointer or keyboard inspection highlights an hour sector and opens an animated floating time/value tooltip; the time clock includes minutes and hours.
- Average streams and minutes per calendar day, including dates without saved plays.
- Daily and Cumulative charts, each showing streams and minutes together. Single-day ranges, including Day and same-date Custom, use the clocks' 24 Eastern wall-clock hourly bins. Daily plots per-hour values at bin midpoints; Cumulative has 25 points, from zero at midnight through end-of-hour running totals to the full day's total at the next midnight. Multi-day ranges retain daily bins, or monthly bins above 730 dates. Inspection shows a crosshair, highlighted points, and a floating date/hour/value bubble rather than a separate subtitle. Chart data disclosures have been removed; the focusable SVG supports inspecting every plotted row with Left/Right arrows and Home/End.
- Streams by weekday, month, and year. A year bar remains visible when the selected data contains only one year; no year section is rendered when there are no year rows.

Top opens artwork-led rankings with Artists selected. Tracks, Artists, and Albums each support listening-time or stream-count sorting through a funnel Filter disclosure. Its text appears at extended widths and hides at narrower widths. One layout button cycles three columns, four columns, five columns, then list. Before an explicit choice, the grid follows the viewport: five at extended widths, four at normal widths, three at compact widths. Thirty results appear initially; Show more adds thirty. Track links open Spotify where available. Artists use actual cached Spotify profile images with `object-fit: contain` to preserve framing, Spotify artist links, and a Spotify attribution link. Missing images use neutral initial placeholders, never album covers; Tracks and Albums retain album artwork. Empty rankings show a timeframe-specific message.

The public ranking projection includes the union of the top 200 entities by time and top 200 by count for each ranking kind. It does not expose an unlimited catalog. Ties use name ordering.

## Evidence and interpretation

The UI's **streams** are saved playback entries, not verified complete listens or a duration-qualified stream definition. Track identity follows provider identity, so different versions can remain distinct. Artist collaborations credit each artist; artist totals overlap. Overall listening counts each entry once. Distinct album totals require known album IDs; rankings can retain unknown albums.

Time aggregation prefers actual duration when available, then falls back to song length; unknown duration contributes no invented time. Recent Spotify records provide song lengths, so minutes, hours, days, and time rankings can overstate listening after skips or pauses. The public payload retains machine-readable `timeBasis`, and time tiles, minute charts/clocks, and ranking values retain title-tooltip provenance. There is no measured-only selector or qualification-threshold control in this surface.

Records belong to their playback timestamp's Eastern date/hour; estimates are not distributed across elapsed clock intervals. Empty dates mean no saved records, not confirmed silence. Period comparisons do not establish equal collection coverage.

Hourly charts reuse the existing saved hour aggregates through `src/lib/music-chart-series.ts`; repeated daylight-saving hours aggregate into the same wall-clock bucket. The chart represents 24 clock labels, not a promise that every selected date lasts 24 elapsed hours. No API, database, or collection change accompanies this display correction.

The protected snapshot query selects the latest 25,000 saved plays. Its truncation flag becomes the public payload's `partial` flag, without a visible warning panel. Earlier records can remain stored while summaries, Lifetime, earliest date, and rankings describe only the returned snapshot. This cap and pending imports limit completeness.

## Access and data path

The public route reads a saved snapshot through the protected collector function's dashboard action, then serializes an explicit aggregate allowlist in `src/lib/music-public.ts`. The browser receives summary and previous-period totals, date-level series, hourly/weekday/month/year aggregates, freshness, range metadata, provenance/partial flags, and ranking metadata. It receives no individual plays, private record IDs, per-play timestamps, tokens, or collector controls. Requested range dates and collection freshness are intentionally public metadata.

`MUSIC_COLLECTOR_KEY` and `MUSIC_SUPABASE_PROJECT_ID` are server-only configuration. For local development the route can fall back to the ignored `private-data/music/collector-bootstrap.json`; do not commit that file or credentials. No public Supabase RPC was introduced. The protected snapshot RPC remains behind the server and collector action.

The server caches the saved snapshot for 60 seconds. Successful aggregate responses use `Cache-Control: public, max-age=30, s-maxage=60`; validation and unavailable responses use `no-store`. A visible open page rerequests saved statistics every 60 seconds. Dashboard reads use stored history and artist metadata only, with no Spotify API calls. The private collector refreshes artist metadata after committing history, using a seven-day cache. Operational details are in `music-collector.md`.

## Visual inheritance and responsive behavior

The surface keeps the site's Manrope, warm charcoal, dusty red, page chrome, background field, cursor, and transitions. Numbers, charts, and artwork carry its hierarchy. The user-supplied stats.fm screenshots guide composition and functionality within the existing visual world.

Local exceptions are intentional: rounded metric tiles, quiet charcoal content grouping, compact controls and type, a sticky range bar, and rounded artwork/list surfaces. These implement the explicit dashboard reference; they do not replace the site's open foreground rule or become global tokens. Metric tiles use four columns at wide widths and two below 1000px. Daily/cumulative and weekday/month chart pairs stack below that breakpoint. At compact widths, the title shrinks, Stats/Top use icons with accessible names, and the range controls wrap. Month bars fit the viewport without horizontal scrolling; compact labels abbreviate to initials while accessible labels retain the full month and value.

Controls expose labels and pressed states. The custom disclosures animate in place, close after selection, outside pointer interaction, or focus departure, and return focus to their trigger on selection or Escape. Closed panels are inert. Clocks and line charts have accessible names, visible focus styles, associated tooltips, and live readouts. Left/Right arrows inspect hours or chart rows; Home/End select the first/last value; Escape dismisses the highlight. Bar labels and values remain text in the document. Custom date inputs retain visible focus styling. Reduced-motion disables dashboard transitions and tooltip animation. These features do not by themselves constitute a full accessibility audit.

The navigation decode experiment adds mostly opaque charcoal Voronoi cells and thin muted-red seams, fading each cell with its matching reveal sector. It retains the existing 620ms decode timing, reduced-motion bypass, and CSS crossfade fallback. This interaction refinement does not establish new global visual tokens.
